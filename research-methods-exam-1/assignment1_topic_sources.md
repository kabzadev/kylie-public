• **Topic:** The dependent variable is **youth/adolescent aggression** (i.e., aggressive behavior among adolescents). The three independent variables are: (1) cyberbullying victimization, (2) social media use, and (3) association with delinquent/aggressive peers.

---

• **IV1: Cyberbullying Victimization**

  ○ Source 1: Hussain, Z., Kircaburun, K., Savcı, M., Griffiths, M. D., & Pourrazavi, S. (2023). The role of aggression in the association of cyberbullying victimization with cyberbullying perpetration and problematic social media use among adolescents. *Journal of Concurrent Disorders*, 5(1), 1–15.
    - Hussain et al. (2023) found that cyberbullying victimization was significantly and positively associated with aggression among adolescents, and that aggression mediated the relationship between cyberbullying victimization and subsequent cyberbullying perpetration. This suggests that being a victim of cyberbullying directly increases aggressive behavior in youth.

  ○ Source 2: Guo, S. (2016). A meta-analysis of the predictors of cyberbullying perpetration and victimization. *Psychology in the Schools*, 53(4), 432–450. https://doi.org/10.1002/pits.21914
    - Guo (2016) conducted a meta-analysis of 77 studies and found that cyberbullying victimization had a significant positive association with externalizing behaviors, including aggression and rule-breaking behavior, among adolescents. The effect size was moderate, indicating a meaningful link between experiencing cybervictimization and engaging in aggressive conduct.

---

• **IV2: Social Media Use**

  ○ Source 3: Lin, S., Fabris, M. A., & Longobardi, C. (2025). The association between social media addiction and aggressive behaviors: A longitudinal and gender-specific analysis. *Journal of Adolescence*, 97(2), 456–470. https://doi.org/10.1002/jad.12454
    - Lin et al. (2025) conducted a longitudinal study and found that social media addiction was a significant predictor of aggressive behavior in early adolescents over time. The effect was significant for both genders, though stronger among males, supporting the hypothesis that excessive social media use contributes directly to youth aggression.

  ○ Source 4: Lin, S., Longobardi, C., & Gastaldi, F. G. M. (2024). Social media addiction and aggressive behaviors in early adolescents: The mediating role of nighttime social media use and sleep quality. *The Journal of Early Adolescence*, 44(3), 345–368. https://doi.org/10.1177/02724316231160142
    - Lin et al. (2024) found that social media addiction had a significant direct effect on aggressive behaviors among early adolescents, and that this relationship was partially mediated by nighttime social media use and poor sleep quality. The findings suggest that both the amount and timing of social media use are important predictors of youth aggression.

---

• **IV3: Association with Delinquent/Aggressive Peers**

  ○ Source 5: Lin, S., Yu, C., Chen, W., Tian, Y., & Zhang, W. (2018). Peer victimization and aggressive behavior among Chinese adolescents: Delinquent peer affiliation as a mediator and parental knowledge as a moderator. *Frontiers in Psychology*, 9, Article 1036. https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2018.01036
    - Lin et al. (2018) found that affiliation with delinquent peers significantly mediated the relationship between peer victimization and aggressive behavior among adolescents. Specifically, adolescents who associated with more delinquent peers exhibited higher levels of aggression, and this effect was stronger when parental knowledge of adolescent activities was low.

  ○ Source 6: Tu, Z., Cui, Y., Zhang, W., & Luo, F. (2025). Peer influence and selection impact on adolescent aggression: Exploring nonaggressive delinquency, peer victimization, and moral disengagement. *Journal of Adolescence*, 97(1), 112–128. https://doi.org/10.1002/jad.12501
    - Tu et al. (2025) used stochastic actor-oriented models (SAOMs) to examine how peer networks influence adolescent aggression over time. They found that both peer influence (adopting aggressive behaviors from peers) and peer selection (aggressive youth choosing similar friends) significantly predicted increases in adolescent aggression, confirming that association with aggressive peers is a robust predictor of youth violence.
