# Kylie Criminology Career Report

## Executive Summary

For a rising FSU Senior majoring in Criminology, entry-level career paths offer diverse opportunities across law enforcement, private sector security, social services, and legal support. Top picks for new graduates include Police Officer (versatile, average starting $50,000, high demand in Florida), Probation Officer (lower stress, $45,000, stable federal/state roles), Cybersecurity Analyst (growing field, $70,000, private sector), Fraud Investigator ($50,000, analytical skills), and Paralegal ($45,000, legal stability). Overall, roles in federal agencies and security show strong growth, while considering geographic factors like demand in FL/TX/CA and avoiding low-pay/high-stress areas.

## 2. Salary Data

Salaries are approximate starting averages for entry-level positions, drawn from BLS (10th-25th percentiles), Indeed, and ZipRecruiter. Actual pay varies by location, experience, and employer. For example:

## 3. Geographic Analysis

### Hot Spots (High Demand States/Regions)
States with high employment density, growth, and pay:
- Florida (FL): High demand for law enforcement and security; proximity to FSU; average pay $68,400; employment per 1,000: 0.13.
- Texas (TX): Border roles, high growth; pay $77,620 in metro areas.
- California (CA): Tech security roles; pay $79,330.
- New York (NY): Urban law enforcement; pay $65,000.
- North Carolina (NC): Highest density (0.43 per 1,000); pay $54,490.

Other: Ohio, New Jersey, Pennsylvania, Virginia, Maryland, Louisiana.

### Places to Avoid
States with low pay relative to cost of living, high stress, or low demand:
- Georgia: Low pay ($71,610 median for investigators), high stress in urban areas.
- Mississippi: Low opportunities, pay below national average.
- Rural states like Wyoming/Montana: Few jobs, low population density.
- High-cost states like Hawaii: High pay ($121,000) but extreme cost of living; stress from tourism/security.
- Avoid high-crime urban areas (e.g., Chicago, Detroit) for safety, despite job availability.

Stress: Law enforcement roles are high-stress/dangerous; private/social roles lower risk.
Safety: Private sector generally safer; federal roles require clearances.

Sources: Criminal Justice Degree Hub (https://www.criminaljusticedegreehub.com/top-states-for-jobs-in-criminal-justice/), BLS (https://www.bls.gov/oes/), All Criminal Justice Schools (https://www.allcriminaljusticeschools.com/criminal-justice/criminal-justice-salaries/).
