Kylie Criminology Career Report

Executive Summary

For a fresh FSU Criminology graduate, non-patrol, non-sworn entry-level roles in forensic science & crime scene, forensic psychology & social work, and intelligence & analysis offer specialized opportunities focusing on evidence handling, victim support, and data-driven investigations. Top picks include Forensic Technician ($40,000-$50,000, high growth in labs), Crime Analyst ($45,000-$55,000, analytical focus), Victim Advocate ($35,000-$45,000, supportive role), Probation Officer ($40,000-$50,000, stable social services), and Intelligence Analyst ($50,000-$60,000, federal demand). These paths leverage criminology skills in low-stress, specialized environments, with strong prospects in major forensic hubs like CA, NY, FL, and TX.

1. Career Paths for Fresh Criminology Graduates

These roles build on criminology knowledge of criminal justice systems, evidence protocols, behavioral psychology, and data analysis. Fresh graduates typically enter via internships, on-the-job training, or certifications, advancing through experience or further education.

Forensic Science & Crime Scene

  • Forensic Technician: Collect and process crime scene evidence, document scenes, and assist in lab analysis. Entry through bachelor's + training; strong growth (13-14% through 2033).
  • Evidence Custodian: Manage evidence storage, chain-of-custody logging, and retrieval in agencies/labs. Requires procedural knowledge; overlaps with technician roles.
  • Fingerprint Technician: Develop and classify fingerprints from scenes or labs. Involves specialized training; certification recommended.
  • DNA Lab Assistant: Assist in DNA sample preparation and analysis under supervision. Needs biology focus; stepping stone to full analyst.
  • Latent Print Examiner: Examine and identify invisible prints; provide court testimony. Advanced training and certification required.

Forensic Psychology & Social Work

  • Victim Advocate: Support crime victims through legal processes, provide resources, and offer emotional aid. Focuses on advocacy and case coordination.
  • Behavioral Health Technician: Assist in mental health support for offenders or at-risk individuals, monitoring behaviors and facilitating treatment.
  • Youth Counselor: Counsel at-risk youth in juvenile justice or prevention programs, develop plans, and connect to services.
  • Probation/Parole Officer (Social Services track): Supervise offenders, create rehabilitation plans, and link to resources like counseling or housing.
  • Court Liaison: Coordinate between courts, defendants/victims, and agencies; handle intake and support in legal settings.

Intelligence & Analysis

  • Crime Analyst: Analyze crime data, patterns, and trends to inform policing and policy. Involves statistics, GIS, and reporting.
  • Intelligence Analyst: Evaluate threats, criminal motivations, and intelligence for law enforcement or agencies like DHS.
  • Investigative Assistant: Support detectives with research, evidence collection, and case preparation.

2. Educational Requirements

A Criminology bachelor's suffices for most roles, but some require additional certifications or a master's for advancement. Roles needing biology/science courses or master's are noted.

Forensic Science & Crime Scene

  • Forensic Technician: Bachelor's in criminology/forensic science; criminology ok with science courses. Certs: ABC/IAI (optional but recommended).
  • Evidence Custodian: Bachelor's in criminology/forensic-related; criminology suffices. Certs: Evidentiary proficiency (ABC/IAI).
  • Fingerprint Technician: Bachelor's in criminology/forensic science; partial with impression training. Certs: IAI Latent Print.
  • DNA Lab Assistant: Bachelor's in biology/forensic science; criminology insufficient without biology. Certs: ABC Molecular Biology.
  • Latent Print Examiner: Bachelor's in forensic science; criminology partial. Certs: IAI Latent Print.

Forensic Psychology & Social Work

  • Victim Advocate: Bachelor's in criminology; suffices. Certs: NACP (recommended).
  • Behavioral Health Technician: Bachelor's in criminology; suffices, but healthcare certs helpful. Master's in psychology preferred for advanced.
  • Youth Counselor: Bachelor's in criminology/social work; suffices. Certs: Child welfare; master's preferred.
  • Probation/Parole Officer: Bachelor's in criminology/social work; suffices, state training. Master's boosts advancement.
  • Court Liaison: Bachelor's in criminology; suffices. Certs: Victim advocacy.

Intelligence & Analysis

  • Crime Analyst: Bachelor's in criminology/criminal justice; suffices. Certs: CLEA/CICA (optional).
  • Intelligence Analyst: Bachelor's in criminology/data analytics; suffices. Master's enhances prospects.
  • Investigative Assistant: Bachelor's in criminology; suffices. No specific certs required.

Sources: Various forensic and criminal justice education sites.
